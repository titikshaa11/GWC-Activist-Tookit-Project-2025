Feeling stuck?

Find some resources below to help.

A simple yet effective way to calm yourself down is through finger touch breathing, a technique that combines breath control with light physical touch to increase focus and relaxation. Start by gently touching the tip of your thumb to the tip of your index finger as you inhale slowly, then switch to your middle finger as you exhale. Continue this pattern through each finger—inhale with the ring finger, exhale with the pinky, then reverse back.
If you're feeling emotionally stuck, distracting your mind with a creative or sensory activity can be a powerful tool. Try coloring, listening to calming music, organizing a small space, going for a walk, or even doing a simple puzzle. These actions gently redirect your attention, offering your brain a break from distressing thoughts.
When emotions feel overwhelming, one of the most effective ways to reset is through deep breathing. Try the 4-7-8 technique: inhale quietly through your nose for 4 seconds, hold your breath for 7 seconds, and exhale slowly through your mouth for 8 seconds. Repeat this cycle 3–4 times. This method helps slow your heart rate, calm your nervous system, and create a sense of grounding.
Another helpful method is progressive muscle relaxation. This technique involves tensing and releasing different muscle groups in your body, one at a time. Start at your toes and work your way up to your head, squeezing each muscle tightly for a few seconds before relaxing it. When you're upset or on edge, it can help shift focus away from racing thoughts and promote physical calmness.
